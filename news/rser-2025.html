Systematic Review Published in Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews

"Systematic review on uncertainty quantification in machine learning-based building energy modeling" published in Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 218, 115817.

Authors Xinyue Xu, Yuqing Hu, Sez Atamturktur, Li Chen, Julian Wang
DOI 10.1016/j.rser.2025.115817

Abstract

[ assets/images/news/rser-2025.jpg ]
UQ taxonomy overview from the systematic review — RSER 2025

Uncertainty quantification (UQ) has received increasing attention for improving the reliability, transparency, and robustness of machine learning (ML)-based building energy modeling (BEM). As ML methods are widely integrated into BEM applications, there is a growing need for a structured and comprehensive understanding of UQ implementations in ML-based BEM. This paper addresses this gap by presenting a thorough review of literature at the intersection of ML, BEM, and UQ, guided by a systematic literature search using a Keyword Synonym Search strategy.

Three primary sources of uncertainty are identified in ML-based BEM: building system operations, building simulation tools, and ML models. The review examines ten state-of-the-art UQ techniques, including ensemble modeling, prior networks, Bayesian neural networks, MCMC, variational inference, dropout networks, Bayes by Backprop, Bayesian active learning, variational autoencoders, and Gaussian processes.

Key Contributions

  • 01 Systematic mapping of three uncertainty sources (aleatoric vs. epistemic) across building system operations, simulation tools, and ML models.
  • 02 Comprehensive review of ten state-of-the-art UQ techniques and their practical applications within ML-based BEM.
  • 03 Identification of five research challenges, including underrepresentation of aleatoric uncertainty in building datasets and limited adoption of advanced UQ methods.
  • 04 Recommendations for developing uncertainty-aware ML-based building energy models to support reliable energy analysis and decision-making.
